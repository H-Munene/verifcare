Generated by `scripts/run_domain_shift_robustness.py`.

Primarily descriptive - the same 'report it, don't force a verdict' treatment Phase 7 gave ECE.

- **clean**: ROC-AUC=0.6427 (0.0614), F1=0.5269 (0.0421)
- **bandpass_only**: ROC-AUC=0.6199 (0.0499), F1=0.4580 (0.0702)
- **bandpass_white_20db**: ROC-AUC=0.5766 (0.0853), F1=0.3621 (0.0398)
- **bandpass_white_10db**: ROC-AUC=0.5464 (0.0755), F1=0.3231 (0.1105)
- **bandpass_pink_20db**: ROC-AUC=0.5950 (0.0643), F1=0.4053 (0.0568)
- **bandpass_pink_10db**: ROC-AUC=0.5750 (0.0784), F1=0.4053 (0.0494)

**Bandpass-only alone drops performance meaningfully, with zero added noise** - ROC-AUC 0.6427 to 0.6199, F1 0.5269 to 0.4580. A clean, independent finding validating the decision (section 9e) to test bandpass as its own condition rather than only combined with noise - the channel-response change alone is doing real work here, not just the noise conditions layered on top of it.

## Realized vs. nominal SNR (noise conditions only)

Measured directly from the generated audio (via the bandpass filter's linearity, not assumed equal to the nominal target used to scale the noise before mixing).

- bandpass_pink_10db: nominal=10dB, measured=14.96dB (SD=0.75)
- bandpass_pink_20db: nominal=20dB, measured=24.93dB (SD=0.76)
- bandpass_white_10db: nominal=10dB, measured=10.42dB (SD=0.58)
- bandpass_white_20db: nominal=20dB, measured=20.42dB (SD=0.58)

**White noise tracks its nominal label closely; pink noise drifts ~5dB milder.** The passband (100-7000Hz) on 16kHz audio (Nyquist 8000Hz) excludes only a small slice at each end of the spectrum. White noise's energy is flat across the full range, so that small excluded slice removes a similarly small, proportional share of its total power - hence its realized SNR stays close to nominal. Pink noise's 1/f spectrum concentrates disproportionate energy at the low end specifically, right at and below the 100Hz cutoff, so the same cutoff removes a much larger share of pink's total power than of white's - leaving less pink noise in-band than its nominal label assumed, and making its realized SNR come out higher (milder) than intended.
**This means the pink-vs-white comparison in the table above, at matched *nominal* labels, is not a fair comparison of actual severity** - pink@10dB was, in practice, a milder (~15dB realized) condition than white@10dB's true ~10.4dB. The mechanistic question of whether pink genuinely hurts this model more than white at *matched realized severity* is not resolved by this table; see the exploratory follow-up below.

## Pre-registered formal test (the only one Phase 9 runs)

**bandpass_pink_10db vs. clean**, paired by fold, on ROC-AUC: mean diff (worst-clean)=-0.0676, t=-3.240, p=0.0317 (family size 1, no correction) - **significant**.
ROC-AUC: clean=0.6427, worst-case=0.5750. F1 (clean-derived threshold): clean=0.5269, worst-case=0.4053.
Pre-registered before any condition was run (section 9e) - not selected after seeing results, and not redone despite the realized-SNR finding above: the condition as actually run was, if anything, milder than the intended 10dB (measured ~15dB), yet still produced a significant, fold-consistent drop (4 of 5 folds declining). That is arguably a more conservative, more defensible result than a correctly-calibrated one would have been, not a weaker one - stated plainly rather than walked back.

**Scope**: this checks XGBoost's specific robustness, not "the pipeline's" robustness in general - a different model selected in Phase 8 could show a different degradation curve.

## Exploratory follow-up: pink noise corrected for realized-SNR drift

**Not part of the pre-registered design or its one formal test** - run() above stands exactly as run; this answers the mechanistic pink-vs-white question the main run's realized-SNR finding left open, without redoing or reweighting the confirmatory result.
Pink @ nominal 10dB was re-run with an exact analytic correction for the realized-SNR drift measured in the main run (not an empirically averaged offset - bandpass filtering is linear, so the drift a given noise draw picks up from filtering is fully predictable from one extra filter pass and cancelled exactly). Uses a freshly-drawn noise realization (same RANDOM_SEED, independent stream), not a rescaling of the original pink@10dB run's exact noise instances.

- **bandpass_pink_10db_corrected**: realized SNR=10.00dB (SD=0.00, target 10dB), ROC-AUC=0.5400 (0.0656), F1=0.4098 (0.0475).
  For comparison: clean ROC-AUC=0.6427.

**Read cautiously, not as a confirmation**: corrected pink (ROC-AUC=0.5400, SD=0.0656) is nominally the lowest point estimate of any condition in this phase, lower than white @ its own true ~10.4dB (ROC-AUC=0.5464, SD=0.0755) and lower than the original miscalibrated pink@10dB (~15.0dB realized, ROC-AUC=0.5750) reported in the main table above.
But the gap between corrected pink and white@10dB is only 0.0063 - smaller than nearly every gap this project has treated as inconclusive elsewhere (Phase 6's XGBoost tuning gain, a fold-consistent 4/5-fold effect, didn't clear two-tailed significance at this same n=5 folds), and smaller than either condition's own fold-to-fold SD. This comparison was not pre-registered and no formal test was run on it. The honest reading: corrected pink is **consistent with, not confirming**, the mechanistic hypothesis that pink hurts this model more than white at matched severity - a genuinely interesting exploratory point estimate, not a settled finding.
White noise was not itself re-run through this correction - its drift (~0.4dB) was already judged negligible in the main run, not because the correction mechanism is pink-specific: `degrade()`'s `correct_realized_snr` handling has no noise-type-specific logic and would apply identically to white noise if used.
