## Tuned vs. Phase 5's untuned baseline - paired per fold, not two independent means

Phase 5 and Phase 6 share the exact same 5 outer folds (re-verified byte-identical), so this is a fold-by-fold paired diff (tuned AUC minus Phase 5's AUC on that same fold), not a comparison of two aggregate means - pairing cancels out whatever makes a given fold's subject composition easier or harder for both numbers alike, the same discipline this project applies to between-model comparisons (the one-SE rule) applied here to a before/after comparison.

With only 5 paired folds (df=4), the correct reference distribution for these numbers is Student's t, not a normal approximation - the two-tailed alpha=0.05 critical value at df=4 is ~2.776 (scipy.stats.t.ppf(0.975, 4)), not ~1.96. Reported below as a real paired t-test (scipy.stats.ttest_1samp of the diffs against 0), not an "X SE from zero" framing, which implicitly invites the wrong (normal) reference.

- **random_forest**: improved in 3/5 folds; mean paired diff=-0.0036 (SD=0.0112, SE=0.0050); t(4)=-0.713, two-tailed p=0.5150, one-tailed p=0.7425.
- **svm**: improved in 2/5 folds; mean paired diff=-0.0228 (SD=0.0586, SE=0.0262); t(4)=-0.870, two-tailed p=0.4335, one-tailed p=0.7833.
- **xgboost**: improved in 4/5 folds; mean paired diff=+0.0295 (SD=0.0280, SE=0.0125); t(4)=+2.352, two-tailed p=0.0783, one-tailed p=0.0392.

**XGBoost**: t(4)=+2.35, two-tailed p=0.078 - does not clear conventional two-tailed significance at this sample size. It does clear a one-tailed test (p=0.039, critical t=2.132), defensible only if "tuning helps" is treated as a directional hypothesis rather than tested post hoc - stated here explicitly rather than left implied. Combined with fold-consistency (4/5 folds improved, by a similar-sized margin each time, not one lucky fold), this is genuinely suggestive of a real improvement, but not confirmed at n=5 - read as directionally promising, not proven.

**Random Forest**: t(4)=-0.71, p=0.515 - not distinguishable from no change. Improved in 3/5 folds yet the mean diff is negative: the 2 worsening folds (-0.0154 each) outweighed the 3 improving folds (+0.0005, +0.0092, +0.0033), which is why more-folds-improved and a negative mean aren't a contradiction - stated explicitly rather than left for a reader to reconcile. Genuinely flat, not "slightly worse."

**SVM**: t(4)=-0.87, p=0.433 - also not distinguishable from no change, and *less* confidently negative than it first looks: SVM's diffs have the largest SD of the three, and a larger spread at a similar t-magnitude to Random Forest's is evidence of *less* confidence, not more (that is exactly why SE=SD/sqrt(n) exists). The internally consistent reading is that SVM's tuning result is **unstable and inconclusive**, not a confident negative finding - and this is the version actually consistent with the mechanism below (which predicts instability, not a reliable negative effect).

A plausible, general-ML-knowledge mechanism for this pattern (not a repo-specific claim): gradient boosting (XGBoost) is well known to be considerably more sensitive to its hyperparameters (learning rate, tree depth, number of estimators) than Random Forest, whose bagging-based defaults tend to already be robust general-purpose choices - and at this sample size, SVM's inner training set (~110 subjects, further split by the 3-fold inner CV) may simply be too small for a hyperparameter search to reliably beat sensible defaults without overfitting the inner-CV signal itself, consistent with SVM's much larger paired-diff SD (instability), not with a confident negative effect.

Tuning is not free of its own optimism risk even with nested CV done correctly, and n=5 folds is underpowered for strong claims either way. Reported plainly: Random Forest is flat, SVM is inconclusive, and only XGBoost shows a directionally-promising (not statistically confirmed) improvement.
